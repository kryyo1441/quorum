A question

You don't. Today the answer is: because the server said so.

Why signatures are not enough

Many attacks were selective.

Researchers reproducing it saw nothing.

They were served the clean file, like everyone else.

A valid signature proves who signed, not who else got it.

If the attacker holds the server or the key, the signature says nothing about the other 99.9% of users.

The idea

Make every release public.
Make the public agree.

1 · Log
An append-only public log

Every release is a leaf in a Merkle tree. History cannot be rewritten without everyone noticing.

2 · Witnesses
Independent cosigners

They check the log only ever grows, then sign its head. They gossip to catch a log showing different people different things.

3 · Client
Verify before install

Install only if k of n witnesses vouch for this exact file. Anything unreachable or wrong: fail closed.

Honest limits

What this does not do.

Detected, not prevented.

A stolen-key release is accepted until the monitor's next poll.

Collusion defeats it.

k witnesses plus the log. Our demo witnesses are all ours: independence is the next milestone.

A fork must be shown to someone who verifies.

That client forwards the head; a fork nobody sees is not provable.

No key rotation or revocation yet.

After a stolen-key alert the vendor cannot retire the key in-band.

Freshness is on witness heartbeats.

A log can freeze its head; independent witnesses and expected narrow, not remove, that.

Telemetry privacy.

Observations are pseudonymous, which reduces but does not remove the concern.
